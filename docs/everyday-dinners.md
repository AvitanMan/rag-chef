# Everyday dinner collection

This first set focuses on familiar meals with preparation included from the starting
ingredients. It adds eight original MIT-licensed recipes to the three existing
starters. These are authored recipes, **not kitchen-tested recommendations**. No
total cooking time, nutritional value, or dietary certification is invented.

| Dinner | Main ingredients |
| --- | --- |
| Garlic Tomato Pasta | Pasta, canned tomatoes, garlic, Parmesan |
| Creamy Garlic Mushroom Pasta | Pasta, mushrooms, cream, Parmesan |
| Paprika Chicken & Potato Tray Bake | Chicken breast, potatoes, onion, paprika |
| Chicken & Pepper Rice Bowls | Chicken breast, rice, pepper, canned tomatoes |
| Egg & Pea Fried Rice | Uncooked rice, eggs, peas, carrot, onion, soy sauce |
| Golden Potato & Onion Frittata | Eggs, potatoes, onion |
| Tuna & Tomato Pasta | Pasta, canned tuna, canned tomatoes, garlic |
| Tomato Skillet Eggs with Toast | Eggs, canned tomatoes, onion, garlic, bread |

The table is a summary, not a shopping list. Each recipe includes the complete
measured ingredient list, supporting seasonings and cooking directions for two.
Chicken and egg doneness guidance references
[FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).
Step-level checking times are not verified start-to-finish totals.

Open **Recipes → Everyday dinners → Add everyday dinners** to add the collection.
Fresh installations use **Load starter recipes**. Both use the existing durable,
idempotent starter job and original-content source policy. Existing imported recipes,
pantry, plans and favorites are retained. The evaluation corpus is not imported.

Cards explain the dish and show pantry matches and missing ingredients. Each
pantry dinner option uses at least one picked food and honors saved exclusions;
it can use a different subset of your picks than another option. Other pantry
food supports the recipe but does not introduce unrelated meals. Ranking favors
fewer missing foods, then more picked foods, after dinner role and dislikes.
Salt, black pepper, water and a small explicit set of cooking oils are supporting
ingredients; overlap in these alone does not produce a dinner option, and the
app never assumes they are available.
Authored appliance requirements apply to the exact bundled versions; no total time
or dietary certification is inferred. Existing explicit metadata reviews take precedence.
Ingredient identity stays conservative: cooked rice is distinct from uncooked rice,
and chicken breast is not silently replaced by another cut. Unknown amounts do not
prevent a name match, but a match does not prove there is enough to cook.

The older Basic Lasagna is retained in the wider catalog. Its source review does
not establish cooking quality: it depends on separately prepared sauce and noodles
and includes whipped egg whites. It is not part of this everyday collection.

After cooking, use Favorites for meals worth repeating. The next useful content
decision is based on a few actual cooking trials: which dish tasted good, whether
the steps worked, which ingredients caused extra shopping, and what should change.
Do not promote these candidates as tested favorites before that feedback exists.
